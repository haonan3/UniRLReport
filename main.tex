\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement-learning systems share an outer loop---generate, score, assign credit, and update---yet their implementations fragment because autoregressive tokens, diffusion trajectories, multimodal values, and multi-stage rollouts carry different state and impose different execution constraints.
We present \system, a trajectory-centric system that places a lineage-preserving, modality-aware intermediate representation at the boundary between post-training semantics and distributed execution.
A trajectory batch is a sequence of typed layers, one per depth of a forest of prompt-rooted trees, carrying decoded values, model conditions, replay segments, rewards, and behavior-policy versions.
A rollout request is a skeleton of such layers whose generated layers are still empty shells, and every engine fills them through one $\sample\!\rightarrow\!\sample$ transformation.
The same trajectory can carry autoregressive and diffusion stages linked by lineage, while logical roles, placement, tensor transport, weight publication, and memory residency determine how the fill executes.
These contracts factor model-specific replay from lineage, movement, placement, and scheduling decisions; they do not assert that the underlying objectives or every engine--topology combination are interchangeable.
The design is grounded in a source audit of UniRL release v0.2.0 (commit \texttt{fd344ad}); subsequent experiment reports provide initial GPU evidence.
Three-seed diffusion results show improved held-out automatic scores together with reduced within-prompt diversity, while an eight-GPU native-recipe comparison reports lower iteration time than VeRL-Omni.
A separate, single-run, recipe-matched 500-step Qwen3-4B DRPO comparison records 12.3\% higher end-to-end output-token throughput for UniRL than veRL while generating 6.3\% longer responses.
A source-importing CPU harness exercises ten core IR invariants.
The present evaluation distinguishes legacy report-derived measurements from the newly regenerated AR systems evidence: the primary AR quality endpoint, real-rollout lineage validation, paired representation cost, and a controlled time-to-quality result remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Online post-training is a feedback program.
It expands inputs into policy samples, evaluates those samples, turns feedback into a learning signal, and applies one or more optimizer updates.
At this level, language-model GRPO, diffusion-model policy gradients, prompt-rewrite pipelines, and tool-using agents look deceptively similar.
Their data paths do not.
An autoregressive update may need packed token IDs, masks, rollout log probabilities, and per-token values; a diffusion update may need a sparse set of latent states, a noise schedule, and transition statistics; a composed workflow must retain which image descended from which rewritten prompt; an asynchronous run must additionally retain which policy version generated every sample.

Existing systems have made major progress on scalable LLM post-training and flexible device mapping~\citep{sheng2024hybridflow,hu2024openrlhf,wang2025roll,nemorl2025}.
Fully or partially asynchronous systems address rollout tail latency and accelerator idle time~\citep{fu2025areal,zhang2026relax,slime2025}.
Meanwhile, diffusion RL methods and multimodal frameworks broaden the optimization and model families that can be trained~\citep{black2023ddpo,liu2025flowgrpo,xue2025dancegrpo,zheng2025diffusionnft,verlomni2026}.
The remaining design question is how the information shared by these workflows should survive changes in their model-specific state and execution paths.

\begin{quote}
\emph{Can one post-training program cross generative model families and execution topologies while retaining the structure needed for correct grouping, replay, credit assignment, transport, and policy-version control?}
\end{quote}

Its answer is to make a trajectory intermediate representation (IR) the stable semantic object, then expose execution choices as explicit contracts around that object.
The IR makes a complete prompt-rooted lineage, rather than an individual row or an LLM-specific tensor dictionary, the unit of scheduling and sharding.
Typed leaves distinguish decoded multimodal values from model conditions and optimization segments.
Distributed methods declare how each structured field scatters, concatenates, packs, or remains shared.
Dense tensors can be replaced by row-addressable references without changing the trajectory structure.
This representation is passed through a small set of logical roles and can be mapped to colocated or disjoint GPU slabs.

The paper develops two coupled design ideas:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A \emph{lineage-preserving trajectory IR} in which a rollout request is a typed tree skeleton that can span autoregressive and diffusion stages, each generated layer is conditioned through its ancestry and keeps its own replay segment, and training folds reward and credit back along the same tree (Section~\ref{sec:trajectory}). Synthetic equality checks provide initial semantic evidence; recorded-rollout validation and the primary AR quality endpoint remain open alongside the reported diffusion results.
  \item A \emph{factorization of semantic roles from physical execution}: rollout remains one $\sample\!\rightarrow\!\sample$ fill of that skeleton, while field-aware dispatch, tensor references, placement, weight publication, and residency specify its execution (Section~\ref{sec:execution}). Native-recipe, recipe-matched AR, and internal topology comparisons provide initial performance observations; the paired representation-cost control remains unmeasured.
\end{itemize}

Asynchronous execution is where the second idea pays off most directly.
Because rollout, reward, and training are logical roles rather than fixed steps of one loop, the orchestration layer---admission, root-complete grouping, version stamping, lag filtering, and weight publication---can be lifted out of individual trainers.
UniRL's AR and diffusion trainers therefore share one bounded-staleness manager over the same trajectory IR (Section~\ref{sec:async}), and we exercise it on both workloads.
For AR, single-seed capacity measurements with overlap enabled show lower per-rollout time than synchronous colocated rollout; the accompanying quality pilot did not pass, so no time-to-quality benefit is claimed (Section~\ref{sec:rq4}).
For diffusion, denoising and replay compute dominate each iteration and leave little generation idle time for overlap to recover, so asynchrony yields no significant gain.

\section{Problem: Preserve Semantics While Changing Execution}
\label{sec:problem}

Let an online post-training iteration consume a batch of roots $X$, sample trajectories with behavior policy $\pi_v$, obtain reward $r$, construct credit $A$, and update parameters $\theta$:
\begin{equation}
X \xrightarrow{\pi_v} \mathcal{T}
  \xrightarrow{R} (\mathcal{T},r)
  \xrightarrow{C} (\mathcal{T},A)
  \xrightarrow{U_\theta} \theta'.
\label{eq:outer-loop}
\end{equation}
The compact notation hides three forms of heterogeneity.

\paragraph{Trajectory heterogeneity.}
The sufficient statistics for an update depend on the generative process.
For AR policies, a segment may be a variable-length packed token sequence with masks, old log probabilities, values, and returns.
For stochastic diffusion or flow policies, a segment may contain selected latent snapshots, a shared noise schedule, the indices of sampled transitions, and rollout or replay transition statistics.
Multi-stage and agentic paths add nonuniform lineage and observations.

For example, let prompt $p$ produce rewrites $w_{p,j}$ and images $x_{p,j,k}$.
The image model conditions on $w_{p,j}$, but credit may compare either images under that rewrite or all images under $p$.
A flattened image batch can implement both choices only if it retains or reconstructs the relevant parent keys.
This example motivates explicit ancestry, while the image model's replay state motivates typed segments; neither requirement follows from the other.

\paragraph{Role heterogeneity.}
Generation, reward evaluation, replay, gradient computation, and policy publication differ in runtime, parallel layout, and lifecycle, not only in kernels.
Rollout may run inside the training process or in a dedicated serving engine with its own tensor-parallel layout, such as SGLang~\citep{zheng2024sglang}, vLLM~\citep{kwon2023vllm}, or vLLM-Omni~\citep{yin2026vllmomni}, while a sharded training backend such as FSDP~\citep{zhao2023fsdp} or VeOmni~\citep{ma2025veomni} owns parameters and optimizer state.
Reward ranges from an in-process scorer to an environment-isolated scorer process or a remote multi-model service.
These roles must agree on when each copy of the policy is resident, offloaded, or published, and publishing from a sharded trainer is itself a collective over the whole training mesh.
Which boundary dominates cost depends on the configuration: in the measured separate-slab deployment, the report attributes much of the slowdown to reward processing (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}).

\paragraph{Topology heterogeneity.}
A small run may time-share one GPU set between training and rollout; a larger run may separate them; a variable-duration rollout may benefit from asynchronous admission.
Each choice changes memory pressure, transfer paths, synchronization frequency, and allowable policy lag.

These observations produce four requirements (Table~\ref{tab:requirements}).
The first two follow from trajectory heterogeneity and the third from role and topology heterogeneity; the fourth specializes the third to asynchronous execution, where decoupled roles may train on work generated by an older policy.

\paragraph{Non-goals.}
\system does not erase model-specific code; it confines it to a model bundle---architecture-specific stages, conditions, and rollout adapters---and to an algorithm that defines its loss over replay.
It does not automatically discover an optimal placement.
The present runtime uses a driver to sequence roles and Ray actors~\citep{moritz2018ray} to host them; it is not a new cluster scheduler.

\section{A Trajectory-Centric Programming Model}
\label{sec:trajectory}

\subsection{A trajectory batch is a sequence of tree layers}

The central type is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T} = [P_0,P_1,\ldots,P_L],
\qquad
P_i = (I_i, X_i, C_i, Z_i, R_i, A_i, V_i, M_i),
\label{eq:sample}
\end{equation}
where $I_i$ are sample identifiers, $X_i$ decoded modality values, $C_i$ model conditions, $Z_i$ optimization segments, $R_i$ rewards, $A_i$ advantages or returns, $V_i$ the generating engine's policy version (behavior-version provenance only on the asynchronous paths; Section~\ref{sec:lifecycle}), and $M_i$ auxiliary metadata.
In code, $\mathcal{T}$ is a \sample{} and each $P_i$ is a batched \parttype{}, rather than a single tree node.
The sequence encodes a forest of root-prompt trees: $P_i$ holds every node at depth $i$, and edges connect adjacent layers.
We call the last layer the \emph{frontier}; trajectories grow by appending layers to it.
A generated layer is typed by the sampling-parameter class that produced it and the segment class it carries, for example an AR layer with a \texttt{TextSegment} or a diffusion layer with a \texttt{LatentSegment}; input layers carry the prompts and observations that condition generation.

Identifiers encode ancestry.
Root IDs contain no lineage separator; a branch appends a child ordinal.
For every non-root row $j$ in $P_i$, its parent ID must occur in $P_{i-1}$:
\begin{equation}
\forall i>0,\;\forall s\in I_i:\quad \operatorname{parent}(s)\in I_{i-1}.
\label{eq:parent-invariant}
\end{equation}
The constructor checks Equation~\ref{eq:parent-invariant}.
The \texttt{fork} operator appends $k$ generated children per row of the frontier; \texttt{observe} appends a branch-one input from an environment; and \texttt{propagate\_rewards} reduces child reward to an unscored parent.

Batch operations act on complete root groups.
\texttt{Sample.split()} returns one tree-complete trajectory per root, and slicing or selection is implemented by selecting these groups rather than independent rows.
Moving complete roots preserves the parent-contiguous order constructed by \texttt{fork}; independently permuting child rows need not preserve the credit computation.

\paragraph{Well-formed batches.}
Lineage operations are defined over well-formed trajectory batches: root IDs are unique, trajectories being merged have the same number of parts, and siblings follow the parent-major order produced by \texttt{fork}.
The programming model establishes these properties by construction---trajectories grow only through \texttt{fork} and \texttt{observe}, and batches are sharded and selected as complete root groups---so credit assignment and reward reduction can consume each sibling group as one contiguous block.
Group-advantage computation rejects batches whose siblings are not contiguous, part concatenation requires matching decoded-value metadata across shards, and the asynchronous batch assembler admits only batches generated by a single behavior version.
E0 is the gate that checks these properties at the actual experiment boundaries (Appendix~\ref{app:correctness}).

\subsection{Values, conditions, and segments are separate layers}

\system distinguishes three kinds of leaves that are often conflated.

\paragraph{Primitives.}
Primitives are decoded or externally supplied values: text, images, videos, audio waveforms, and media references.
Image pixels and variable-duration video/audio values use packed representations with per-example shapes or cumulative offsets.
They are suitable for reward functions, visualization, and subsequent turns, but need not be the tensors replayed by the trainable model.

\paragraph{Conditions.}
Conditions are model-facing encodings, such as text embeddings or image latents.
They allow rollout adapters and train-side stages to agree on typed inputs without prescribing one tokenizer, text encoder, or multimodal fusion architecture.

\paragraph{Segments.}
Segments retain optimization-facing state.
\texttt{TextSegment} stores variable-length packed token fields.
\texttt{LatentSegment} stores latent snapshots at selected indices, a shared sigma schedule, and optional SDE log probabilities or transition means.
The sparse index map is important: a diffusion algorithm can replay only selected transitions instead of materializing an entire denoising path for every loss.

The distinction makes decoded payload lifetime independent of replay lifetime.
After reward logging, trainers may drop large decoded media while retaining conditions, segments, reward, and credit for training.

\subsection{A field algebra defines structural batching}

Every structured batch field carries a kind: concatenated, packed variable-length, or shared (the default for an unannotated field); scalar reductions by maximum, minimum, sum, or mean are also available but are applied only when concatenating, and the current source uses only the maximum, for timing metadata.
Concatenation, row selection, slicing, and repetition act recursively on concatenated and packed fields and pass shared fields through unchanged; device movement applies to every tensor regardless of kind.
The same kinds define how a distributed method reconstructs its return value.
For example, a DP-scattered \sample{} is split by root groups (the root count must be divisible by the data-parallel size), and the data-parallel heads' results are concatenated position-wise; another argument whose leading size differs from the split size is replicated to every shard rather than rejected.
A broadcast lifecycle method returns the list of per-worker results, and callers that expect a replicated value check agreement explicitly.

This removes hand-authored tensor slicing from each role; merging shards relies on the well-formed-batch properties above.
It also creates a measurable cost: recursive structural operations and reference bookkeeping must be benchmarked in Section~\ref{sec:rq3} rather than assumed free.

\subsection{Rollout unfolds a declared trajectory tree}
\label{sec:unfold}

A rollout request is the skeleton of the tree it will become.
Starting from the root layer, the caller appends one generated layer per stage with \texttt{fork}; until an engine fills it, such a layer is a \emph{shell}.
Each shell fixes its child count, carries child identifiers that already name their parents, and holds a typed sampling-parameter object---autoregressive or diffusion---that identifies the generative process that must fill it.
The prompt-enhancement and unified-model trainers build
\begin{equation}
\texttt{request}(P_0).\texttt{fork}(N,s^{\mathrm{AR}}).\texttt{fork}(M,s^{\mathrm{diff}})
= [P_0,\;P_1^{\mathrm{AR}},\;P_2^{\mathrm{diff}}],
\label{eq:skeleton}
\end{equation}
where $s^{\mathrm{AR}}$ and $s^{\mathrm{diff}}$ are the stages' sampling parameters, and whose $BN$ AR rows and $BNM$ image rows, for $B$ root prompts, are fixed before any token or latent is sampled.
Every rollout engine implements one transformation over such a skeleton:
\begin{equation}
F_{\theta,e}: \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{skeleton}} \longrightarrow \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{filled}},
\label{eq:engine}
\end{equation}
where $e$ identifies a train-side or external generation runtime.
$F$ keeps the request's part sequence and identifiers and writes decoded values, conditions, and a replay segment into the shells it fills, and stamps its policy version on the frontier it returns; when an engine or trainer fills several shells through child engines, it copies each child's version onto the shell that child filled.
The base contract also exposes lifecycle and policy-publication operations, but it does not standardize the engine's internal serving API.

\paragraph{One trajectory spans autoregressive and diffusion stages.}
The filled skeleton records every stage of a multi-stage generation in the trajectory itself.
Within a pipeline that fills several shells, a generated shell obtains its inputs from its ancestry: \texttt{conditioning\_at} walks each row's parent chain and collects the role-tagged primitives along it.
In the HunyuanImage-3 and BAGEL text-to-text-and-image modes, one pipeline fills the AR shell from the root prompt; the image shell's lineage then surfaces both the original prompt and the generated reasoning as its conditioning, without a side table that pairs each image with the text that produced it.
Each stage's replay state lands in its own layer---a \texttt{TextSegment} from an AR stage and a \texttt{LatentSegment} from a diffusion stage---so the filled tree carries what credit assignment and replay need for both stages (Section~\ref{sec:fold}).
An AR stage and a diffusion stage are thus two typed layers of one prompt-rooted tree rather than two batches joined after the fact.
The representation carries this cross-stage dataflow; stage order is declared by each workflow's skeleton.
The current trainers instantiate single-stage AR, single-stage diffusion, input--AR--diffusion, and multi-turn tool trajectories from the same representation.

\paragraph{Engine forms.}
The current source contains three direct forms of Equation~\ref{eq:engine} and one coordinator that composes it.
The train-side adapter invokes the model pipeline directly under evaluation mode, filling every generated shell of the request.
Dedicated serving adapters for SGLang, SGLang-Diffusion, vLLM, vLLM-Omni, and FastVideo translate typed trajectory inputs to their native requests and rebuild typed responses.
A composed engine serially fills the AR and diffusion shells of Equation~\ref{eq:skeleton} with two child engines.
An agentic harness composes Equation~\ref{eq:engine} over turns: it forks a policy turn, has a named engine fill it, and appends the environment observation before the next turn, returning a longer part sequence.
The claim is therefore about a common boundary, not one universal generation kernel.

\subsection{Training folds the tree into per-stage updates}
\label{sec:fold}

Training traverses the filled tree in the opposite direction.
The reward role scores the frontier, which in the cross-stage layouts above is the image layer.
\texttt{propagate\_rewards} then reduces each scored layer onto its unscored parent layer by mean, maximum, or sum, so an upstream AR layer receives feedback from the images it conditioned.
Credit is computed per trainable layer: \texttt{compute\_advantages} labels each row by its ancestor at a chosen depth---the immediate parent by default, or the root prompt when requested---and, by default, normalizes rewards within each group.
For the image layer of Equation~\ref{eq:skeleton}, the two choices compare images under one rewrite or all images under one original prompt; selecting between them is a lineage query rather than a reconstructed join (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}).
Reduction and grouping consume siblings as contiguous blocks, the well-formedness property stated above.
Advantages are attached on the driver before the batch is scattered, so training shards receive precomputed credit rather than re-deriving groups.

Each trainable layer then becomes a training track bound to a train stack and a stage algorithm.
The trainer selects the layer by its sampling-parameter type and skips frozen layers.
In the composed prompt-enhancement workflow, the AR and diffusion models have separate stacks and optimizer steps; freezing the rewrite model, as E4 does, trains only the diffusion track.
When both layers belong to one backbone, as in HunyuanImage-3, a unified stack back-propagates the AR and image losses through one shared optimizer step.

\subsection{The algorithm boundary preserves specialized replay}
\label{sec:algorithm-boundary}

Within a track, the algorithm consumes its layer's conditions, segment, and advantages.
A train stack owns microbatch planning, gradient accumulation, global loss normalization, gradient clipping, optimizer steps, and per-update aggregation.
An algorithm object owns the stage-specific map
\begin{equation}
(C,Z,A;\theta) \longmapsto \mathcal{L}\quad\text{and backward}.
\label{eq:algorithm}
\end{equation}
The loss itself sees only its layer's conditions, segment, and advantages; an optional per-shard preparation hook may read the layer's whole part (for example, per-row metadata or critic values), and some algorithms additionally hold a backend handle for reference or EMA weights.
Before repeated optimizer updates, the stack can freeze an old-policy anchor at the same microbatch geometry used for training.
AR objectives replay token log probabilities; diffusion objectives replay selected transitions and may compare rollout statistics, a pre-update replay, or a reference model.
Supervised and teacher-distillation objectives explicitly opt out of advantages; the forward-process DiffusionNFT objective still consumes group advantages.

Because models and algorithms meet only at this data boundary, they compose without pairwise adaptation.
A model is integrated once, as a bundle whose stages fill and replay typed segments; an algorithm is written once, as a loss over a stage's replay of those segments.
A shared algorithm can then be paired with any model whose stages produce and replay the segment fields it consumes---AR objectives with \texttt{TextSegment} producers and diffusion objectives with \texttt{LatentSegment} producers---so supporting $M$ models and $N$ shared algorithms requires $M+N$ integrations rather than $M\times N$; a model that needs a specialized objective adds it as a subclass without changing the others.
Variants that keep the FlowGRPO loss and change only the SDE transition or the step schedule, such as DanceGRPO and MixGRPO, are recipes rather than new algorithm classes.

This division is deliberately not advertised as algorithmic novelty.
GRPO follows the group-relative formulation introduced for language reasoning~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath}; PPO follows proximal policy optimization~\citep{schulman2017ppo}; diffusion objectives build on prior diffusion-RL methods~\citep{black2023ddpo,liu2025flowgrpo,xue2025dancegrpo,zheng2025diffusionnft}.
The systems question is whether these objectives can consume the same trajectory and execution contracts without hiding their different sufficient statistics.

\subsection{Logical roles, device slabs, and worker slots}

A \texttt{DevicePool} creates one Ray worker per GPU initially and reserves one strict-packed placement group per node.
Each worker occupies a slot on its GPU: slot~0 exists from startup, further slots are created on demand up to a per-GPU limit, and every slot reserves an equal fraction of that GPU.
A top-level placement scope claims a fraction of the whole device pool from the still-unclaimed devices; a nested scope takes a fraction of its parent's devices.
Roles created inside a scope either share its worker slot---enabling local sibling access and memory time-sharing---or receive isolated slots on the same devices (isolated slots require the per-GPU store transport).
Disjoint top-level scopes form separate training, rollout, or reward slabs.

A role is a regular Python object derived from \texttt{Remote}.
The handle constructs one role replica on each selected worker and derives data-, tensor-, pipeline-, sequence-, and expert-parallel rank metadata from the role configuration.
Method decorators declare a dispatch mode (broadcast, scatter, or data-parallel scatter) and which ranks execute; the dispatch mode determines how results are collected.
The trainer is consequently a single-controller program, while each method may invoke multi-controller distributed training or serving inside a role.
This resembles HybridFlow's separation between orchestration and distributed computation~\citep{sheng2024hybridflow}; UniRL's paper claim centers instead on carrying the same typed lineage through heterogeneous generative stages.

\subsection{Tensor references decouple structure from movement}

In the single-controller design, every role method is a remote call issued by the driver, and its result returns to the driver before being dispatched to the next role.
If trajectories carried dense tensors across these hops, latent snapshots, token log probabilities, and decoded media would pass through the driver on every call, and the driver would have to materialize them merely to split, select, or scatter complete root groups.
UniRL therefore routes control through the driver but keeps tensor data on the workers.
When a role method returns, its worker stores every dense tensor leaf in a local transport and \emph{dehydrates} it into a \texttt{TensorRef}: an ordered list of half-open row spans over opaque handles, plus shape, dtype, and device metadata.
The driver receives only this dehydrated trajectory; selection and contiguous slicing construct new span views without moving tensor data.
Before the next call, the transport localizes each reference for its destination worker, leaving resident spans in place and transferring the others worker-to-worker.
The destination worker then \emph{hydrates} all referenced spans in one batch and writes the tensors back into the nested object in place before invoking the method; spans whose trailing widths differ are zero-padded to a common width.
Control decisions on the driver need only small fields such as rewards, which it hydrates on demand.

The transport interface has a deliberately small contract: store tensors, resolve handles, identify references, and optionally localize references for destination workers.
The audited source provides (i) a colocated per-worker store, (ii) a per-GPU store with IPC and NCCL transfers, and (iii) a TransferQueue path backed by simple storage or Mooncake-compatible storage.
Mooncake itself was proposed for disaggregated serving~\citep{qin2024mooncake}; here its storage interface is a transport option rather than a claimed UniRL invention.

The driver can therefore manipulate trajectory structure and metadata without materializing all GPU tensors.
Whether this reduces memory and transfer cost in practice is RQ3, not an assumption.

\subsection{Residency lifecycles and policy publication}
\label{sec:lifecycle}

Training and generation often hold different copies or formats of a policy.
In every deployment, a due publication completes before the generation it serves, and the engine stamps each generated frontier with its current version.
Trainers that publish through the rollout manager set that version to the optimizer version (Section~\ref{sec:async}); the remaining cases are described below.
How publication interleaves with memory residency depends on whether the two roles share devices.

\paragraph{Colocated slabs.}
When training and rollout time-share one GPU set, the engine is put to sleep---releasing its weight and cache memory---between generations and woken before the next one, and training state may be offloaded to host memory while the engine is awake.
Every colocated order keeps two invariants: a due publication completes before generation, and memory is released before the role that needs it is brought up, so the engine sleeps before offloaded training state is restored.
The default order wakes the engine, publishes if due, offloads training state if enabled, and generates; afterwards it sleeps the engine and restores the training state.
The AR and agentic trainers follow this order, and the AR trainer adapts it to engine capabilities: an engine that can restore its weight memory separately from its cache receives weights before allocating the rest, and an engine pinned to one GPU of the slab receives weights that are extracted before training state is offloaded and pushed after the engine wakes (Appendix~\ref{app:residency}).
The diffusion trainers express residency as one resident-or-offloaded choice per role (training, rollout, reward): they offload every non-resident role before waking the engine, publish into the awake engine, sleep it after generation unless the rollout role is resident, and restore an offloaded trainer only before its optimizer step.
The prompt-enhancement trainer follows the default order, and the unified-model trainer with separate AR and diffusion engines uses the same extract-then-push order.
A train-side engine reads live model objects, so training state is never offloaded for it.

\paragraph{Separate slabs.}
When training and rollout occupy disjoint devices, generation needs no memory handoff:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item at startup, connect the training side to the rollout workers through a one-time handshake;
  \item at each publication point, transfer parameters across slabs, under the admission discipline of Section~\ref{sec:async} for asynchronous trainers;
  \item generate and stamp the frontier with the engine's current version.
\end{enumerate}
Generation never offloads training state to make room for the engine.
The synchronous diffusion trainer sleeps the engine between rollouts unless the rollout role is resident and, when the training role is not resident, offloads training state while a reward role shares the training slab.
The asynchronous trainers keep the engine awake; the asynchronous diffusion trainer also requires a resident reward role.

Weight-sync implementations cover in-process and cross-slab LoRA adapter pushes and full-weight paths via serialized tensors, CUDA IPC (ZMQ or vLLM's native weight-transfer engine), SGLang checkpoint-engine IPC, NCCL broadcast, and a checkpoint-file handoff.
The base engine can also receive per-track updates for composed models.

The stamped policy version is behavior-version provenance only on the asynchronous paths.
The asynchronous and agentic trainers set it to the optimizer-update count at each publication.
The synchronous trainers do not advance it, so their frontiers keep version zero, except that the vLLM engine stamps the number of weight transfers it has received through vLLM's native weight-transfer engine.

The source rejects combinations whose lifecycle has not been validated, such as a train-side rollout on a physically separate slab or selected EMA/offload combinations.
This fail-closed policy is important because a partially awakened engine or silently stale adapter can produce plausible but invalid training data.
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} therefore includes replay-consistency and failure-injection checks alongside throughput.

\subsection{Synchronous execution}

The synchronous AR trainer performs rollout, reward, credit assignment, replay/update, optional evaluation, and checkpointing in order.
The diffusion trainer can accumulate multiple rollouts into one optimizer window and can place reward on the train slab or a third slab, or score micro-batches inside the colocated rollout window so that reward overlaps generation.
Topology is a placement choice made through the same scopes for every modality.
The synchronous AR trainer builds one colocated slab, optionally pinning the engine to one of its GPUs, and the synchronous diffusion trainer additionally builds separate training and rollout slabs.
Separate-slab AR without overlap is the asynchronous trainer with one prompt batch in flight, publication after every batch, and zero allowed lag (Section~\ref{sec:async}): it admits the next batch only after publishing the weights produced from the previous one, so generation and training strictly alternate.
In every case, topology changes resource ownership, residency, and the publication path, but not the trajectory boundary: the same \sample{} passes from rollout through reward and credit to the train stack.
This makes a controlled topology experiment possible: hold model, prompts, group size, reward, number of replayed steps or tokens, and optimizer updates constant while changing only engine or placement.
The publication path and training data-parallel width change with the placement and are reported as part of it.

\subsection{Bounded-staleness execution}
\label{sec:async}

Asynchrony changes correctness conditions, not just control flow.
UniRL's asynchronous AR and diffusion trainers run on separate training and rollout slabs and share one manager.
The driver submits whole prompt batches only while they fit the in-flight capacity, the freshness horizon implied by the staleness allowance, and the batches remaining before the next evaluation, checkpoint, or final boundary.
A background dispatcher then fills every engine slot up to a configured per-worker capacity.
Completed trajectories are grouped by root; only complete groups enter the ready buffer.
Every generated part must carry an output policy version $v_o$.

Let $v_t$ be the current optimizer-update version, which counts committed optimizer steps, and $u$ the configured number of optimizer updates per consumed rollout batch.
The manager exposes lag in update units,
\begin{equation}
\ell = v_t-v_o \leq u b,
\end{equation}
where $b$ is the staleness allowance in rollout-batch units (configurable for AR, fixed to the publication interval minus one for diffusion).
The filter measures each trajectory by its oldest generated part and discards whole root groups beyond that budget, including incomplete roots held across a publication.
Because a skipped non-finite step does not advance $v_t$, admitted work can be more than $b$ rollout batches old.
Before publishing version $v_t$, it pauses admission, drains or suspends work, completes any started or batch-aligning prefix, checks that no publication splits a training batch across behavior versions, publishes weights, updates the rollout version, and then resubmits eligible pristine carry.
Evaluation and checkpointing require a fully empty manager.

This discipline differs from claiming fully asynchronous optimization.
Weight publication remains a barrier.
The asynchronous diffusion trainer admits one prompt batch at a time and requires each training batch to come from a single generation request, so its next generation overlaps only the optimizer step; the AR trainer may keep several batches in flight and refills before reward scoring.
The evaluation must report the actual lag histogram, dropped work, and barrier time; a nominal ``async'' label is insufficient.

\section{Instantiations of the Common Contracts}
\label{sec:instantiations}

The following paths show which information crosses the common boundary and which computations remain specialized.
They illustrate the contracts at the design level, as implemented in the audited source, rather than inventory the system's capabilities.
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} exercises only the group-relative AR objectives, FlowGRPO, and the frozen-rewrite cross-stage path; the GAE, DiffusionNFT, rewrite-training, and agentic paths described below have stock recipes but no reported training runs.

\subsection{Autoregressive reasoning}

An input part carries prompt text and metadata.
Forking by group size creates generated shells with sibling IDs.
An AR engine fills packed token segments and decoded text, and a reward role attaches scalar or component rewards.
Group-normalized credit is computed on the driver before the batch is scattered; token-level generalized advantage estimates are instead computed per trajectory on the training workers, after the critic anchor is frozen.
The train stack replays the same segment and applies the selected objective.
Optional token-length balancing permutes individual generated rows of the training layer before the train stack scatters them, after driver-side group credit but before per-trajectory GAE, which does not depend on row order.
It keeps each row's identifier and any attached advantage but abandons sibling contiguity, so no grouping operation may follow it; the E3-AR UniRL recipe enables it.

\subsection{Diffusion and flow generation}

A diffusion request forks prompts and records sampling parameters, selected SDE indices, and an initial-noise recipe; the hosting engine pins the sigma schedule onto the shell before generation.
The engine returns decoded media plus a latent segment whose sparse trajectory fields are sufficient for the selected update.
FlowGRPO-style objectives replay recorded steps and compare new transition log probabilities against a frozen rollout or replay anchor~\citep{liu2025flowgrpo}.
Forward-process objectives such as DiffusionNFT use clean generated samples rather than the same reverse-trajectory likelihood path~\citep{zheng2025diffusionnft}.
The common train stack handles microbatching and optimizer state; the algorithm remains responsible for these incompatible estimators.

\subsection{Cross-stage and environment-interacting trajectories}

In a prompt-enhancement path, one root produces one or more AR rewrites, and every rewrite produces one or more images (Equation~\ref{eq:skeleton}).
The image reward is attached to the leaf; when the rewrite stage trains, parent-major reduction supplies its feedback.
The primary E4 case freezes that stage and uses ancestry to select the diffusion advantage groups.
The prompt-enhancement workflow needs this ancestry to survive rollout scheduling and sharding; flattening it into unrelated rows would require rebuilding that correspondence out of band.
In a tool-interacting path, each trajectory is a single-branch chain: the policy emits an action-like text part, the environment appends an observation part, and the process repeats until termination or suspension.
The agentic trainer keeps one \sample{} per trajectory, forms a group from repeated copies of the same root rather than forked siblings, computes group advantages over those trajectories, and trains on their flattened generated turns, each inheriting its trajectory's advantage, so lineage there conditions each turn within a trajectory rather than defining groups or shards.

E4 checks these groups against an explicit ID-based reference on synthetic fixtures, leaving the recorded-rollout check open, and then studies the two grouping objectives in training.

\subsection{RQ3: What does the trajectory abstraction cost and enable?}
\label{sec:rq3}

RQ3 first tests the semantic capability that motivates the IR, then measures its cost.
E4 uses the executable frozen-Qwen3-0.6B rewrite $\rightarrow$ SD3.5 path: eight original prompts, four rewrites per prompt, and eight images per rewrite, or 256 image descendants per rollout; only diffusion trains.
This is a separate recipe from E2: $512\times512$, ten steps, one sampled SDE index among 0--8, $\eta=0.7$, LoRA 16/32, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay zero, clip $10^{-4}$, replay anchor, two updates, and microbatch one.
The primary condition (\texttt{diffusion\_group\_scope=prompt}) computes group-relative advantages across all 32 descendants of one original prompt, while \texttt{diffusion\_group\_scope=rewrite} (the trainer default) groups the eight images under each rewrite.
The two stock prompt-enhancement recipes are not a matched pair: the prompt-group recipe uses eight images per rewrite and a 512-token rewrite limit, whereas the rewrite-scope recipe uses four and 256. Both arms must therefore use the prompt-group recipe with only the grouping key changed, as confirmed by their resolved configurations; both stock recipes also enable \texttt{shuffle\_updates} at the audited release.
Both conditions must serialize redacted root/part/parent IDs, shapes, reward/advantage, replay indices, and group labels; the synchronous prompt-enhancement trainer does not advance output versions, so they carry no behavior-version provenance here.
Invalid lineage remains an E0 fail-fast test rather than an intentionally corrupted quality baseline.

For image $x_{p,j,k}$, the two group keys are $g_{\rm root}=p$ and $g_{\rm rewrite}=(p,j)$.
The intended semantic test requires an ID-joined flat-row reference to reproduce UniRL's group memberships, advantages, and learner inputs on identical recorded rewards and replay tensors, including after root-shard round trips.
The 300-rollout, three-seed training comparison changes the normalization population (32 versus eight images); any quality difference would therefore concern the grouping objective, not isolate the IR representation's effect.

Passing that recorded-trajectory control would establish the grouping semantics on real rollout data; it would not by itself prove better task quality.
The current PickScore request is conditioned by the rewritten text (the default \texttt{prompt\_source: generation}; scoring against the root prompt with \texttt{prompt\_source: original} is available but was not used in E4), so a claim about preserving the user's original intent additionally requires lineage-keyed evaluation of every image against its root prompt.
We therefore report both rewrite-conditioned training reward and frozen HPSv3/ImageReward evaluations joined to each root prompt.
Evaluation generates held-out images at $512\times512$, 40 steps, and guidance 1 with the base and every 50-rollout diffusion checkpoint, using one fixed manifest of four frozen rewrites per root and eight image seeds per rewrite for both conditions.
Worker/adapter RNG initialization, deterministic trainside-AR sampling, real trace dumping, and this held-out generation/evaluation path are E0 prerequisites for a multi-seed E4 result.
If the two correct groupings tie, the experiment still establishes semantic expressiveness but not a quality benefit.

\paragraph{E4 reported results and unresolved input validity.}
E4a reports \EFourChecks/\EFourChecks{} successful checks in each scope and bit-exact agreement with an ID join.
The fixtures are synthetic, so this is bounded semantic evidence rather than the recorded-rollout check specified above.
E4b reports no consistent advantage across three seeds: the differences change sign.
This does not demonstrate equivalence or establish that the effect is zero.
The PR additionally discloses malformed rewrite conditioning: chat markers remain in many outputs and many rewrites exceed the CLIP token window because the intended extraction configuration was not applied.
Those defects can affect both absolute quality and the grouping contrast; a statistically inconclusive outcome does not make them irrelevant.
The measured configuration is retained as an exploratory result, while a corrected, separately versioned pipeline must pass a short health check before further quality comparisons.

\paragraph{E5-R remains unmeasured.}
The intended microbenchmarks separate recursive IR operations and tensor transport from model compute, varying root count, branch factor, tensor payload, token/media lengths, and part depth.
Required outputs include structural-operation time, metadata bytes, dense bytes avoided at the driver, transfer time, and reassembly correctness hashes.

The broader E5 protocol compares the colocated store, per-GPU store, and TransferQueue path where hardware permits.
The current report confirms that the available harness does not implement the required paired representation measurement.
The missing comparison is whole-tree sharding versus explicit ID-joined flat rows on the same recorded payload, device, transport, and operations.
This paired representation slice holds model work, device placement, values, grouping, and replay inputs fixed; driver RSS, bytes moved, operation time, and fraction of iteration cost measure the price of preserving structure.
Semantic errors are caught in E0 and are never timed as valid work.

The minimum topology slice reuses E3's colocated SD3.5 UniRL row and adds the same work with \codepath{sd3\_vllmomni\_lora\_separate.yaml}: four GPUs train and four generate while the total eight-GPU allocation remains fixed.
That recipe carries the stock rather than the E3-aligned settings and the repository has no aligned launcher for it, so the E3 overrides must be applied and archived with the separate arm.
This pair measures two deployment regimes, not a pure transport primitive---training parallelism, residency, and local versus remote LoRA publication change together and require a common phase breakdown before attributing their costs.
Broader same-/cross-GPU and cross-node backend measurements remain E5 extensions.
The reported E5-T times are \ReportedColocatedSeconds{} seconds for shared deployment and \ReportedSeparateSeconds{} seconds for the separate allocation, approximately \ReportedTopologySlowdown{} times longer (Figure~\ref{fig:reported-systems}).

\subsection{RQ4: Exploratory bounded-staleness observations}
\label{sec:rq4}

The prespecified study separated the allocation contrast (colocated synchronous S0 versus disaggregated D0 without overlap) from the onset of overlap (D0 versus A1), followed by publication/lag sweeps.
The report states that the formal sweep was canceled on 2026-09-21; only single-seed capacity measurements and a separate quality-target pilot were completed.
The capacity timings, obtained with evaluation disabled, are planning observations rather than a full-budget, equal-quality comparison and are not used for a headline speedup.

\paragraph{The quality-target pilot did not pass.}
The D0 pilot's reported MATH-500 avg@4 decreased from \ESixQualityEarly{} at rollout 150 to \ESixQualityLate{} at rollout 300, contrary to the preregistered requirement that the later checkpoint improve.
No quality threshold was registered and no time-to-quality result is reported.
Training reward also declined over the later window.
This is a negative outcome for the tested pilot criterion; it neither identifies asynchrony as the cause nor establishes that every bounded-staleness configuration is ineffective.
Two checkpoints are insufficient to locate a precise turning point.

\paragraph{Evaluation variability and requirements for a restart.}
The report's repeated evaluation of one checkpoint found variable prompt-level avg@4 scores despite a relatively small aggregate difference.
That observation is specific to the sampled checkpoint and decoding protocol; it is not a universal noise floor or a substitute for training-seed replication.
A renewed study would first audit scorer behavior, request-level sampling and repeat-evaluation variability; match the synchronous/asynchronous estimator and learner settings; and record complete driver time and buffer admission, rejection, discard, retry, and completion accounting.
The source-audited \texttt{perf/step\_time\_s} alone omits outer-loop publication, evaluation, and checkpoint work.
Only after establishing a suitable learning budget and a separately piloted target should a replicated formal grid support a lag--quality--time conclusion.
The original grid and preflight controls remain documented in \codepath{GPU\_HANDOFF\_README.md}; they are not represented here as completed experiments.

\subsection{Fairness and statistical protocol}

Effective work is matched before wall-clock time: prompts, samples per prompt, decoded resolution or maximum tokens, rollout steps, replayed steps/tokens, microbatching semantics, and optimizer updates.
Primary full-system metrics count all allocated training, rollout, and reward resources on both sides. Reward placement and concurrency must be disclosed; a reward-excluded number is supplementary and carries a separate service-cost report.
We prespecify warmup removal, use at least 20 steady-state iterations for throughput summaries, and report the full distribution rather than a single minimum.
Training summaries use the seed as the statistical unit. The present figures show the available individual-seed summaries; a full release should additionally report justified seed-level uncertainty. Bootstrap intervals over prompts or images must be labeled separately and cannot replace seed uncertainty.
The complete systems release must show all process repetitions and the post-warmup per-step distribution; uncertainty of a speed ratio must use repetitions, not correlated steps, as its independent units.
E3-AR releases its 499-step post-warmup distribution, but has only one process-level run per framework; the older report-derived E3 summaries provide neither the complete per-step distribution nor independently revalidated repetitions.
The final checkpoint and named primary endpoint are fixed in advance; the auxiliary metrics diagnose failure modes and are not alternative endpoints chosen after seeing results.
Any future E6 time-to-quality study must use MATH-500 and a target registered from a separate pilot before the formal grid, bracket first observed crossings by evaluation times, and censor runs that never cross. The canceled study produced no such result.
Elapsed time spans initialization, training, publication, checkpointing, and evaluation; steady-state throughput is reported separately.
All published cells require complete scoring or an explicit missingness analysis; silently averaging only successful evaluator responses is inadmissible.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{LLM post-training systems.}
HybridFlow/veRL combines single- and multi-controller execution and introduces hierarchical APIs for mapping RLHF dataflows to devices~\citep{sheng2024hybridflow}.
OpenRLHF uses Ray, vLLM, and DeepSpeed to schedule the models in an RLHF workflow~\citep{hu2024openrlhf}.
ROLL emphasizes a single-controller abstraction, parallel workers, sample lifecycle scheduling, and flexible device mapping~\citep{wang2025roll}.
NeMo-RL provides scalable training and generation backends and multimodal model support~\citep{nemorl2025}.
Group-relative recipes in these systems~\citep{sheng2024hybridflow,hu2024openrlhf,wang2025roll,nemorl2025} typically record one level of sampling structure---responses grouped under their prompt by a per-prompt identifier or by contiguous sample order---whereas UniRL generalizes that group key to multi-level ancestry encoded in sample identifiers.
These systems already drive distinct generation and training backends from one control loop and colocate or separate those backends as a placement choice; UniRL keeps that split and changes the object the loop schedules.
Scheduling and data-parallel sharding move complete root groups rather than rows, and each generated layer carries the replay segment for its own AR or diffusion stage (Section~\ref{sec:trajectory}).
A flat-row batch can recover the same group memberships by joining on retained parent keys (Section~\ref{sec:problem}); UniRL differs in that sharding, credit assignment, and cross-stage conditioning consume that ancestry in place, so changing the execution path does not require rebuilding the join.
RQ3 specifies the corresponding control: an ID-joined reference on recorded rewards and replay tensors (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}).
To date its agreement check uses synthetic fixtures, and the paired cost of carrying the structure (E5-R) is unmeasured; the single-process CPU regression is not that measurement.

\paragraph{Asynchronous post-training.}
AReaL fully decouples generation and training and controls staleness to improve language-reasoning throughput~\citep{fu2025areal}.
Relax uses service-level separation, a TransferQueue data bus, and configurable staleness for omni-modal post-training~\citep{zhang2026relax}.
slime supports persistent asynchronous rollout generation around a training/rollout/data-buffer loop~\citep{slime2025}.
These systems make asynchrony an execution design space.
UniRL takes a conservative point within it: publication remains a barrier, and lag is bounded in optimizer-update units (Section~\ref{sec:async}).
Each generated part carries its output policy version in the trajectory IR, while admission, lag filtering, and publication stay in one manager shared by the AR and diffusion trainers.
The filter keeps or drops whole root groups so that group-relative baselines stay complete, and no training batch spans behavior versions; this is stricter than AReaL, whose decoupled objective admits trajectories generated under several policy versions~\citep{fu2025areal}.
RQ4 reports only single-seed capacity measurements and a quality-target pilot that did not pass; it supports no throughput or time-to-quality comparison with these systems (Section~\ref{sec:rq4}).

\paragraph{Multimodal and diffusion post-training systems.}
VeRL-Omni extends veRL toward diffusion, unified, and omni-modality models and integrates vLLM-Omni rollout~\citep{verlomni2026}.
Relax is explicitly omni-native~\citep{zhang2026relax}.
Modality coverage therefore does not distinguish UniRL from these systems.
The distinction is how ancestry and typed replay state survive execution: an AR stage and a diffusion stage are two layers of one \sample{} (Section~\ref{sec:trajectory}), and tensor references move their dense state without changing that lineage (Section~\ref{sec:execution}).
Models and algorithms meet only at the per-stage conditions, segment, and advantages that an algorithm consumes, so $M$ models and $N$ shared algorithms require $M+N$ rather than $M\times N$ integrations (Section~\ref{sec:algorithm-boundary}).
E4 runs one frozen AR rewrite stage into a trained diffusion stage (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}).
Its lineage check uses synthetic fixtures, its quality comparison is inconclusive across three seeds, and its rewrite text was malformed; the pending module inventory would list what that path reuses, not measure integration effort.

\paragraph{Diffusion RL algorithms.}
DDPO casts denoising as a multi-step decision process~\citep{black2023ddpo}.
Flow-GRPO converts flow sampling to an SDE and applies group-relative optimization~\citep{liu2025flowgrpo}; DanceGRPO generalizes related ideas across visual generation tasks~\citep{xue2025dancegrpo}; DiffusionNFT instead optimizes a forward-process objective from reward-ranked generations~\citep{zheng2025diffusionnft}.
UniRL consumes FlowGRPO-style and DiffusionNFT objectives through its algorithm interface and claims neither objective (Section~\ref{sec:instantiations}).
What the interface contributes is that their incompatible sufficient statistics stay explicit: FlowGRPO-style objectives replay recorded SDE transitions, DiffusionNFT trains on clean generated samples, and both reuse the same train stack for microbatching and optimizer state.

\section{Limitations and Open Evidence}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Integration cost is per architecture.}
Adding an architecture requires one bundle with its stages, conditions, and rollout adapters; this cost is paid once per model rather than once per model--algorithm pair.
Architecture-specific details such as tokenizers, noise schedulers, latent layouts, and LoRA targets stay inside that bundle and its adapters rather than being abstracted by the trajectory type.
The ordered-layer representation targets trajectories that grow by forking and observation; arbitrary ragged forests are outside its current scope.

\paragraph{Topology is configured, not optimized.}
Placement scopes make resource choices explicit but do not search for the best split.
The best topology depends on model memory, reward cost, rollout variance, interconnect, and publication size.

\paragraph{Asynchronous execution is bounded and partially specialized.}
Publication is a barrier, and current AR and diffusion paths impose different admissible in-flight structure.
Fault isolation is limited by the shared Ray runtime and role lifecycle; the system does not yet claim service-level recovery comparable to dedicated service architectures.

\paragraph{The empirical case is incomplete.}
The GPU evidence supports qualified observations about diffusion scores and diversity, native-recipe iteration time, deployment sensitivity, and one recipe-matched AR throughput comparison.
The AR comparison releases per-step evidence but lacks independent process repetitions and a common external quality endpoint; it therefore does not establish run-to-run uncertainty, training-quality reproduction, or time-to-quality.
The combined evidence also does not establish a quality advantage from lineage grouping, bounded IR overhead, scaling, or a time-to-quality benefit from asynchrony.
Raw GPU records remain unavailable for the legacy report-derived studies, which still need exact run manifests, consistent aggregate provenance, and independent regeneration.
The malformed rewrite pipeline and synthetic-only E4a fixtures further limit the evidence for the central cross-stage claim.

\section{Conclusion}

\system treats post-training as a trajectory program whose semantic object survives changes in generative family and execution topology.
Lineage, typed modality values, replay segments, reward, credit, and policy version live in one IR; rollout, reward, training, transport, and publication are logical roles mapped separately to resources.
This design is most valuable if it passes two empirical tests: credible learning behavior across representative AR and diffusion workloads, and competitive end-to-end execution without weakening semantic parity.
Those tests are necessary but not sufficient for the paper's trajectory-centric thesis: a real composed run must also demonstrate lineage-defined credit/grouping, and the measured IR/topology costs must bound the price of preserving that structure.
The current evidence shows diffusion score improvements accompanied by reduced diversity, faster iteration of one native diffusion configuration, a substantial penalty for the measured separate deployment, and 12.3\% higher end-to-end output-token throughput for UniRL in one recipe-matched AR systems run.
That AR result is a workload-specific throughput observation rather than a repeated-run or time-to-quality conclusion.
The full trajectory-centric claim remains open until real-rollout lineage validation and matched representation-cost measurements complement those observations.
Bounded staleness remains an exploratory operating-region question, with no demonstrated time-to-quality benefit in the canceled study.

\section{Correctness status and remaining checks}
\label{app:correctness}

\paragraph{Executed locally.}
The preliminary RQ3 harness performs ten fail-fast checks on the audited source:
lineage-valid cross-stage construction; exact split/concat; tree-complete select and
slice; mean reward propagation plus group-normalized advantages; rejection of bad
lineage, modality keys, and primitive batch sizes; packed variable-length text
select/concat; and lazy tensor-reference selection, materialization, identity, and
bounds behavior. The structured outcomes and data-sensitive SHA-256 round-trip hash
are stored with the run artifacts.

\paragraph{Required before complete evidence admission.}
The subsequent reports describe E0 correctness closure, but its underlying test and configuration artifacts were not revalidated in this integration.
The following system-level checks are the evidence requirements, not a claim that every experiment still lacks each check:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Rollout--replay parity:} before the first update, compare engine-emitted and train-side replay log probabilities or transition statistics under stated numerical tolerances.
  \item \textbf{Publication parity:} repeat the parity check after each LoRA or full-weight publication path used in an experiment.
  \item \textbf{Batch-version integrity:} verify that an optimizer batch never mixes behavior versions when the configured algorithm assumes one version; this is meaningful only on the asynchronous and agentic paths, whose versions advance at publication.
  \item \textbf{Boundary integrity:} checkpoint and evaluation begin only after the async manager is empty; resume restores the optimizer version and republishes it before new rollout.
  \item \textbf{Work accounting:} compare generated tokens or pixels, denoising and replay steps, scored samples, and committed optimizer updates across systems.
  \item \textbf{Structural preconditions:} validate unique root IDs, equal part counts, parent-major grouping, shared-field equality, and version provenance before merging or propagating credit; check against an ID-joined reference.
\end{enumerate}
