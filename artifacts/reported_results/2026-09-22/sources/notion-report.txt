What the UniRL experiment programme measured, by research question. As of 2026-09-22. Links to every underlying record are at the bottom.

The short version
Diffusion training works — but image variety drops 25%.
AR training is unsettled — DRPO reaches 6.4× its baseline, GRPO ends below its own start, one seed each.
UniRL is 1.73× faster than VeRL-Omni — with a fairness caveat that could shrink it.
The trajectory-IR and async claims are not supported yet — one null, one blocked row, one canceled row.

26,112 images per metric, all three seeds scored, zero errors. The averages are solid; the seed-to-seed spread rests on three points, so per-seed values are shown instead of a standard deviation.
Quality rises on every judge. Diversity falls 25% on every seed — and it is already down by rollout 50, so stopping training early does not avoid it.
This is not reward hacking: the independent judges rise too. It is quality up, variety down.
For you to decide: does the diversity number go inside the RQ1 claim, or beside it?

Run-to-run spread is about 1%, so the gap is far bigger than the noise. FA3 versus SDPA is a tie.
Matched on parameter count, the gap is 1.70× — VeRL trains 27% more LoRA parameters.
The fairness caveat. VeRL made more progress per step: +0.10 reward against UniRL's +0.075. Normalised by progress the gap would fall to roughly 1.2×. We do not think that normalisation is sound at 30 steps, but the paper has to say it rather than leave it to be found.
One easy misreading: VeRL's reported generation time includes scoring, UniRL's does not. Compared like for like it is 1.57×, not the 3.3× the raw rows suggest.
RQ3 — What does the trajectory IR cost and enable?
Grouping correctness (E4a) — passes. 24 of 24 checks in both scopes, bit-exact against a plain ID join. Caveat: the test data is synthetic, not a recorded rollout.
Grouping quality (E4b) — null. Root-scope versus rewrite-scope: the three seeds disagree in sign. The limit is seed count, not prompt quality. Basically the reward collapse
Topology (E5-T) — measured. Splitting into 4 training + 4 rollout GPUs is 2.277× slower than sharing 8. Almost all of it is reward computation, not communication. In that layout UniRL is slower than VeRL-Omni colocated.
Representation cost (E5-R) — not measured. The existing harness measures something else, and the comparison arm has to be written from scratch (multi-day). Either build it, or narrow the paper's cost claim.

Faster per rollout, but the quality did not hold: MATH-500 went 0.798 at rollout 150 down to 0.773 at rollout 300, where the registered rule required an increase. So no quality target was registered.
This is training getting worse, not reward hacking — the training reward falls from about rollout 150 too.
A side finding that constrains every row: scoring the same checkpoint twice agrees on only 76% of prompts (±1.5 accuracy points). Never read per-prompt or per-topic results off a single scoring.
Also worth knowing
The evaluation hang that cost E1 several days is fixed upstream (UniRL#462). Cause: a grader subprocess that refused to die, waited on with no timeout.
Three traps when serving a checkpoint: export_full writes no tokenizer; serving needs --disable-piecewise-cuda-graph; and a virtualenv missing math_verify silently scores every answer 0.
What needs a decision
RQ1 wording — diversity inside the claim, or beside it.
E1's endpoint — score the checkpoints on MATH-500, and decide whether a second seed runs. An idle 4×8 pod is available.
E2-R — run the held-out comparison or not; it needs a pod and a rebuilt reward service.
E5-R — build the missing measurement, or narrow RQ3.
RQ4 — drop the section, or publish the negative result.
E3's fairness disclosure — text only, no new runs.
